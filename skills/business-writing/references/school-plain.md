# The plain school

A copy school of the creative studio. A writer seated under this school writes the piece so that a reader who
stops after the first screen still leaves with the answer. It is one of two schools; a run seats one writer
under each, and neither sees the other's draft.

## The position

The reader came with a question and little time. The piece answers it at once, states each fact with its
figure, and gets out of the way. Trust comes from being checkable, not from being liked.

## What both schools share

These bind every writer of a run alike. No axis below is about them, and no line of this file loosens them.

- **What is true.** A draft states only what the brief's truth pack holds. A fact the pack lacks is written
  `[FACT NEEDED: what is missing]`, never estimated, rounded up or borrowed from memory.
- **The constraints file.** The search intent, the heading outline, the terms to cover, the internal-link
  slots and the length band come from `constraints.md` and are the same for both writers.
- **The reader.** Who the brief says is reading, what they already know, and the level each technical point
  is explained at.
- **The format file.** Where the title and the description go, how headings nest, how a placeholder and a
  link slot are written.

## Axis vocabulary

| Axis | Values |
|---|---|
| `opening` | `answer-first`, `context-first`, `scene-first` |
| `claims` | `figure-led`, `qualified`, `verdict-led` |
| `rhythm` | `even`, `varied`, `staccato` |
| `person` | `impersonal`, `second-person`, `first-person` |
| `proof-placement` | `beside-claim`, `after-argument`, `in-story` |
| `call-to-action` | `up-front`, `at-each-step`, `at-close` |

## Axis table

One value per axis. A writer under this school takes the Value, or an Also allowed value when the brief asks
for it, and writes the choice in the direction contract before drafting.

| Axis | Value | Also allowed | In practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| `opening` | `answer-first` | none | The first paragraph answers the question the reader searched for, in full. Background comes after it, for the reader who wants it. |
| `claims` | `figure-led` | `qualified` | A claim is its figure with its unit and what it was measured on. `qualified` adds the condition the figure holds under, in the same sentence. No judgement stands in for a number the truth pack has. |
| `rhythm` | `even` | none | Sentences of similar, moderate length, one idea each. A paragraph is three or four of them. Nothing is set apart for effect. |
| `person` | `impersonal` | `second-person` | The subject of a sentence is the thing being described. `second-person` is for steps the reader carries out. The writer does not appear. |
| `proof-placement` | `beside-claim` | none | The figure, the test condition and the source sit in the sentence that makes the claim, or the one after it. A table carries anything compared on more than two points. |
| `call-to-action` | `up-front` | none | The one action is stated in the opening section, once it has been earned by the answer, and again as the last line. |

## Process

1. Read `constraints.md`, the brief and the truth pack. Mark which truth-pack entry answers the search
   question: that entry opens the piece.
2. Write the direction contract: the axis table with your value on each axis, then three lines:
   1. the sentence that answers the reader's question, as it will appear;
   2. the claims the piece makes, each with the truth-pack entry it rests on;
   3. what was left out because the reader does not need it to decide.
3. Draft under the heading outline. Under each heading, the point first, then the figure that supports it.
4. Cut a third. Then check every number in the draft against the truth pack by eye.

## Refuse list

| Refused | Why | What this school does instead |
|---|---|---|
| An opening paragraph that sets the scene before it answers | The reader who leaves early leaves with nothing | Open with the answer; the scene, if any, is the second section |
| An adjective doing the work of a figure the pack holds | "Impressively quick" cannot be checked or quoted | The figure, its unit and what it was measured on |
| A rhetorical question used as a section opener | It delays the point by a sentence | State the point |
| A one-line paragraph placed for drama | Emphasis by layout, not by content | Fold it into the paragraph it belongs to |
| The writer's own experience as evidence | It is one case and the reader cannot check it | The measured figure and its source |
| A closing section that restates the piece | The reader has just read it | End on the action and the one fact that supports it |
| A comparison with no figure on either side | "Far ahead of its rival" is a judgement posing as a fact | Both figures, or no comparison |
| A joke that needs the reader to share a reference | It costs the reader who does not | Leave it out |
