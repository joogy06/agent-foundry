# Cover-Letter Patterns

The 4-beat template, worked examples, consistency rules, and the do-not-send list for `career-application-writer`. Read-rate figures are contested and live in `~/.claude/skills/career-coach/references/market-snapshot-2026-06.md`; the craft norms here are evergreen.

---

## The 4-Beat Template (150–200 words, ≤ ½ page)

1. **Hook** — one specific reason for *this* company / role. Never "I am writing to apply for…". Open on something true and particular.
2. **Fit** — one quantified result that maps to their top scorecard criterion. The strongest single proof you belong.
3. **Why this company** — one concrete, current detail (a product, a stated priority, a recent move) that shows you actually looked. This is the beat the CV cannot carry.
4. **CTA** — short, confident close. No groveling, no over-promising.

**Minimum payload across the four beats:** 1 specific company detail + 1 quantified result + 1 lived anecdote.

**Asymmetric-downside framing.** The letter is *never the deciding asset and never sloppy*. Read-rates are contested; rejection-on-the-letter is real (snapshot). Treat it as downside-protection.

## Worked Example 1 — Banking (regulated)

> Your team's note that controls should be "auditable, not just automated" is exactly the line I've worked. Last year I rebuilt the alert-tuning pipeline on our surveillance platform — cut false-positive alerts by about a third and still passed second-line review with no remediation actions. What drew me to <Bank> specifically is your stated push to bring model-risk evidencing in-house rather than lean on the vendor's black box; that's the harder, more durable version of the problem and the one I want to work. I'd welcome the chance to walk through how I'd approach your surveillance backlog.

## Worked Example 2 — Banking (transition into regtech)

> I spent the last four years making trading-desk controls hold up under audit, and your regtech mandate is where I want to take that next. On our reconciliation platform I closed two repeat internal-audit findings and cut manual break-investigation from ~4 hours to under 30 minutes. <Firm>'s focus on shipping controls that survive a regulator's questions — not just a demo — is the reason I'm writing rather than staying put. Happy to talk through the first control I'd prioritize.

## Worked Example 3 — Generalist

> Your job post said the support and engineering teams "don't talk enough" — I've fixed exactly that. At <Company> I put both teams on one weekly triage call and watched ticket reopen-rate drop by about a third in a quarter. What pulled me toward <Company> is that you treat support data as a product signal, not a cost center; that's rare and it's how I already think. I'd love to show you what the first month would look like.

## Consistency Rules

- Company name, your title, dates, and headline metrics must match the CV and LinkedIn exactly (screening tools cross-check — snapshot).
- The letter's one quantified result should *also* appear on the CV — don't introduce a claim that lives only in the letter.
- Voice must match the CV's register and the user's captured voice profile.

## Do-Not-Send List

- A letter that restates the CV in paragraph form (wastes Beat 3).
- A generic letter with the company name find-replaced.
- A letter longer than half a page.
- Any "I'm a perfect fit / hardest worker / passionate about…" filler with no evidence.
- A letter sent against an explicitly "no cover letter" posting.
- Raw model output that hasn't been through the voice + consistency pass.
