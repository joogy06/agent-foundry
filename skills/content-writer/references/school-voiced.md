# The voiced school

A copy school of the creative studio. A writer seated under this school writes the piece as a person with a
view, so that a reader who had no reason to care reads to the end and remembers the verdict. It is one of two
schools; a run seats one writer under each, and neither sees the other's draft.

## The position

The reader has ten tabs open and no duty to this one. The piece earns attention with a point of view, carries
its facts inside an argument, and leaves the reader with a judgement they can repeat. Trust comes from a
writer who commits and then shows why.

## What both schools share

These bind every writer of a run alike. No axis below is about them, and no line of this file loosens them.

- **What is true.** A draft states only what the brief's truth pack holds. A fact the pack lacks is written
  `[FACT NEEDED: what is missing]`, never estimated, rounded up or borrowed from memory. A strong opinion is
  welcome; an invented number is not an opinion.
- **The constraints file.** The search intent, the heading outline, the terms to cover, the internal-link
  slots and the length band come from `constraints.md` and are the same for both writers.
- **The reader.** Who the brief says is reading, what they already know, and the level each technical point
  is explained at.
- **The format file.** Where the title and the description go, how headings nest, how a placeholder and a
  link slot are written.

## Axis vocabulary

| Axis | Values |
|---|---|
| `opening` | `answer-first`, `context-first`, `scene-first` |
| `claims` | `figure-led`, `qualified`, `verdict-led` |
| `rhythm` | `even`, `varied`, `staccato` |
| `person` | `impersonal`, `second-person`, `first-person` |
| `proof-placement` | `beside-claim`, `after-argument`, `in-story` |
| `call-to-action` | `up-front`, `at-each-step`, `at-close` |

## Axis table

One value per axis. A writer under this school takes the Value, or an Also allowed value when the brief asks
for it, and writes the choice in the direction contract before drafting.

| Axis | Value | Also allowed | In practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| `opening` | `scene-first` | `context-first` | The piece opens on a moment the reader recognises: the decision at the checkout, the thing that went wrong. `context-first` opens on what changed and why it matters now. The search question is still answered within the first screen. |
| `claims` | `verdict-led` | none | A section commits to a judgement and then pays for it with the figures. The judgement is the writer's; every figure under it is the truth pack's. |
| `rhythm` | `varied` | `staccato` | Long sentences that carry an argument, broken by short ones that land it. `staccato` is for a passage of quick, parallel points. Read aloud, it sounds like someone talking. |
| `person` | `first-person` | `second-person` | The writer is present and says "I" where a view is a view. `second-person` speaks to the reader's own situation. |
| `proof-placement` | `in-story` | `after-argument` | A figure arrives inside the example or the comparison that gives it meaning. `after-argument` gathers the evidence once the case has been made. |
| `call-to-action` | `at-close` | none | The one action is asked for once, at the end, when the argument has earned it. |

## Process

1. Read `constraints.md`, the brief and the truth pack. Decide the verdict: the one judgement the facts
   support and the reader will repeat.
2. Write the direction contract: the axis table with your value on each axis, then three lines:
   1. the verdict, in one sentence;
   2. the moment or change the piece opens on, and how the search question is answered within the first
      screen;
   3. the claims the piece makes, each with the truth-pack entry it rests on.
3. Draft under the heading outline. Let each section turn: a view, the evidence, what follows from it.
4. Read it aloud. Cut what you would not say. Then check every number in the draft against the truth pack by
   eye.

## Refuse list

| Refused | Why | What this school does instead |
|---|---|---|
| A piece with no judgement in it | A list of facts gives the reader nothing to repeat | Commit to a verdict the facts support |
| An opening that defines the topic | The reader already knows what the thing is | Open on the moment that makes it matter |
| Every sentence the same length | It reads as a manual | Let a short sentence land what a long one built |
| Hedging a view until it is no view | "It may arguably be worth considering" says nothing | Say it, and show the figure that backs it |
| A specification table with no reading of it | The reader came for what the numbers mean | A sentence on what each row changes for them |
| Borrowed catchphrases and a costume voice | An impression of a personality is not a point of view | The writer's own view, in their own words |
| A call to act before the case is made | It asks for trust the piece has not earned | Ask once, at the close |
| Enthusiasm with nothing under it | Excitement about a product is not evidence | The figure that would excite a sceptic |
