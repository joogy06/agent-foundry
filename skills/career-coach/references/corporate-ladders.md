# Corporate Ladder Structures in Finance/Banking IT

> **Banking is the worked example here; the principles generalize.** The title-vs-grade gap, the sponsorship layer above a certain level, and the existence of compensation bands per grade exist in most large organizations — the specific titles and bands differ by sector. Translate the *mechanics* (where the title/grade gap bites, where sponsorship becomes load-bearing, how comp bands constrain negotiation) to the user's actual sector; the banking ladder below is the illustration.

## Banking Title Hierarchies

### Investment Banking Standard (Front Office Benchmark)

All banking divisions map onto this ladder, even when the fit is awkward:

| Level | Title | Typical Tenure | Notes |
|-------|-------|---------------|-------|
| 1 | Analyst (1/2/3) | 2-3 years | Entry level, campus hires |
| 2 | Associate (1/2/3) | 3-4 years | First "career" grade |
| 3 | Vice President (VP) | 3-5 years | **Mid-level, NOT executive** |
| 4 | Director / Executive Director (ED) | 3-6 years | Significant gate; many plateau here |
| 5 | Managing Director (MD) | Terminal for most | Extremely selective; single-digit % make it |
| 6 | Partner / Group Head | Rare | Some firms only |

**Critical:** VP in banking is mid-level. JPMorgan has ~15,000+ VPs. Goldman VP is third rung. Completely different from VP at a tech company.

### IT/Technology Title Mapping

Banks force-fit technology staff onto the banking ladder:

**JPMorgan Chase:**
| Grade | Tech Title |
|---|---|
| Analyst | Technology Analyst / Software Engineer I-II |
| Associate | Associate / Software Engineer III |
| VP | Lead Software Engineer / Engineering Lead |
| ED | Senior Engineering Lead |
| MD | Distinguished Engineer / Department Head |

**Goldman Sachs:** No Director/ED grade — jumps VP straight to MD. The VP-to-MD leap is brutal.

**Citi / HSBC / Deutsche Bank:** Insert AVP (Assistant Vice President) between Associate and VP. AVP is essentially "senior IC who has been around 4-6 years."

### Retail / Commercial Banking Variants

| Grade | Rough IB Equivalent |
|---|---|
| Officer / Banking Officer | Associate-ish |
| AVP | Between Associate and VP |
| VP | VP |
| SVP | Director/ED |
| EVP | MD |
| President / CEO | Beyond MD |

### Fintech Title Structures (Big Tech-Style)

| Level | IC Track | Management Track |
|---|---|---|
| L3-L4 | Software Engineer / Senior SWE | -- |
| L5 | Staff Engineer | Engineering Manager |
| L6 | Senior Staff / Principal | Director of Engineering |
| L7 | Distinguished Engineer | VP of Engineering |
| L8+ | Fellow | SVP / CTO |

**Title translation:** Goldman VP applying to Stripe = roughly L5-L6 (Staff/Senior Staff). Stripe "Senior SWE" applying to a bank = Associate or VP grade.

## IT vs Business Silo Dynamics

### The Cost Center Problem

Revenue-generating divisions (IB, Sales & Trading, Asset Management) are first-class citizens. Technology is historically classified as a cost center alongside Operations, Compliance, HR.

**Consequences:**
1. **Budget cuts hit IT first** when revenue drops
2. **Compensation asymmetry** — VP in IB: $400-700K total comp; VP in Tech at same bank: $200-350K
3. **Organizational subordination** — Tech priorities set by business stakeholders, not technologists
4. **Headcount as "run rate" cost** — every head must be justified

### The Glass Ceiling

- **Up to VP/ED:** Pure technologists advance on technical merit
- **At MD and above:** Must demonstrate "business impact," "client engagement," "revenue enablement"
- **Ways through:** Lead a transformation with visible dollar impact, build something revenue-generating, move into a hybrid business-tech role

### How AI Is Changing This

Technology is becoming revenue-adjacent:
1. AI-driven trading and risk directly generate/protect revenue
2. Client-facing AI products (JPMorgan LLM Suite, Morgan Stanley advisor tools)
3. Automation savings are directly measurable and attributable
4. RegTech is mandated by regulators — teams building it have unusual protection

**AI specialists are breaking through the glass ceiling faster.** Roles like Head of AI and Chief AI Officer are being created at MD+ level.

### C-Suite Power Dynamics

| Role | Reports To | Real Power |
|---|---|---|
| CIO | CEO/COO | Largest headcount, controls budget, "keep the lights on" |
| CTO | CEO/COO/CIO | Strategic influence, often smallest team |
| CDO | CEO/COO/CIO/CRO | Growing but variable; BCBS 239 mandated the role |
| CAIO | CEO/CTO/CDO | Newest; still being defined across the industry |

**Who actually has power:** COO (controls operating budget) > CIO (largest org reach) > CTO (strategic influence) > CDO/CAIO (growing but fighting for permanent seats).

## Promotion Mechanics

### Annual Cycle (Most Large Banks)

| Period | Activity |
|---|---|
| Q1 | Goal setting (OKRs or equivalent) |
| Q2-Q3 | Mid-year review checkpoint |
| Q4 Oct-Dec | Self-assessment → Manager assessment → **Calibration sessions** → Compensation committee |
| Jan-Feb (next year) | Ratings, promotions, bonuses communicated |

### How Decisions Are Actually Made

1. **Sponsorship is #1.** A sponsor (MD+) actively advocates in rooms you're not in. Without a sponsor, your manager's voice alone is insufficient for VP→ED and above.
2. **Visibility and narrative** matter as much as output. Two engineers do identical work; the one who presents at town halls gets promoted first.
3. **"The list"** forms informally months before calibration. Senior leaders discuss promotees in 1:1s, dinners, offsites.
4. **Budget constraints are real.** If 12 deserve promotion but budget allows 8, four get pushed to next year.

### The "Acting Above Grade" Problem

**Why it happens:**
- Cheaper for the bank (Associate doing VP work saves $150K/year)
- "Prove yourself" culture (do the job 1-2 years before getting the title)
- Span of control limits (VP "should manage X people")
- Manager hoarding (holding strong performers back)

**How to fight it:**
- Document everything in competency framework language
- Get the explicit acknowledgment: "Do you agree I'm performing at [next level]?" — in writing
- Set a timeline: "Can we agree I'll be put forward next cycle?"
- **Internal mobility** — lateral move to another team often comes with a grade bump

### Promotion Case Structure

1. **Impact, not activity** — "Reduced infrastructure costs by $2.3M" not "Worked on cloud migration"
2. **Map to competency framework** — get the document from HR, map achievements to each competency
3. **Quantify everything** — revenue, costs saved, risks mitigated (in dollars), headcount, deployment frequency
4. **Stakeholder testimonials** — 3-5 senior stakeholders willing to vouch for you
5. **Show next-level thinking** — strategic proposals, initiatives you started, people you developed

## Compensation Structures

### Base Salary Bands (US Tier 1 City, Large Bank, Technology, 2024-2026)

| Level | Base | Bonus (% of Base, Good Year) | Total Comp Estimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analyst | $85-130K | 10-25% | $95-160K |
| Associate | $130-185K | 20-40% | $155-260K |
| VP | $175-275K | 30-60% | $230-440K |
| Director/ED | $225-350K | 40-80% | $315-630K |
| MD | $300-500K+ | 60-150%+ | $480K-1.25M+ |

Subtract 10-20% for Tier 2 cities. Add 15-25% for fintech equity comp.

### Negotiation Levers (in order of flexibility)

1. **Base salary** — room within bands, especially VP+
2. **Title/grade** — costs nothing, determines future trajectory
3. **Sign-on bonus** — compensates for forfeited comp at previous employer
4. **Guaranteed bonus** — protects against pro-rated first year
5. **Scope** — reporting line, team size, budget authority, strategic projects

## AI Career Paths in Banking

### Three Distinct Tracks

**Track 1: Model Risk Management (MRM)**
- Unique to banking, mandated by regulators (OCC SR 11-7)
- Cannot be outsourced or automated
- Path: Model Validator → Senior Validator → Head of AI/ML Validation → Head of MRM → Chief Model Risk Officer

**Track 2: ML/AI Engineering**
- The builder track
- Most transferable to Big Tech/fintechs
- Path: ML Engineer → Senior → Lead/Architect → Head of AI Engineering → CTO/CAIO

**Track 3: Data Science / Analytics**
- The insights track, closest to business decision-making
- Easiest transition to business roles
- Path: Data Scientist → Senior → Lead → Head of DS for Division → CDO/Head of Analytics

### Regulatory Knowledge as a Career Moat

AI professionals in banking who develop regulatory fluency have a durable competitive advantage:
- **Model Validation (SR 11-7):** Every AI/ML model needs independent validation
- **Explainability (Fair Lending, ECOA, GDPR):** Creates demand for XAI specialists
- **AI Governance (EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF):** Roles like AI Ethics Officer, Head of Responsible AI
- **Operational Resilience (DORA, PRA/FCA):** MLOps/AI Reliability engineers who understand regulation
- **Third-Party Risk (Basel Dec 2025):** AI stacks depend on hyperscalers and model vendors

### The Path to CAIO/CTO

The strongest pathway is NOT "best model builder." It is:
**Platform modernization + resilience + controls + executive operating rhythm.**

Typical trajectory: Automation/Architecture Lead → Enterprise Platform/Data/AI Leader → Accountable Executive

Your unit of value must shift from "solutions I delivered" to "portfolios, standards, funding, and decision rights I shape."

## How AI Is Changing This

Two shifts are reshaping the ladder itself:

- **AI-fluency is now explicit in banking-tech job descriptions** — at most grades, demonstrable AI/automation capability (and the governance to deploy it in a regulated estate) is moving from differentiator to expectation. Plan for it as a core competency (see `career-planning` Framework 6), not a bonus.
- **AI changes the cost-center calculus** — as AI absorbs routine technology tasks, displacement pressure concentrates on roles framed purely as cost. The durable positions are those framed around judgment, accountability, controls ownership, and revenue/risk impact — exactly the moats this ladder rewards at senior grades. The principle generalizes beyond banking: move up the accountability ladder, not deeper into the automatable middle.
