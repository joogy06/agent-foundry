# Tells and the rubric

Two separate judgements. Tells look at ONE variant against what it promised. The rubric compares the variants
against what the visitor came to do. Keep each in its own part of the record.

## Tells: one variant, its own contract

Each maker writes a direction contract before building: one value per named axis (for example type, colour
strategy, density and whitespace, motion, layout structure, imagery, ornament). A tell is a place where the
captured page kept that promise, or broke it.

For each variant, alone:

1. Read its direction contract.
2. Walk its tiles at the three widths.
3. For each axis where the evidence is clear, record a tell: the axis, whether the promise held, and what the
   tiles show. Name the tile when one tile shows it.

Rules:

- A tell never mentions another variant. "Tighter than the other one" is not a tell.
- A tell is never a verdict on the contract itself. A variant that promised dense pages and delivered dense
  pages has kept its promise, whatever the critic would have chosen.
- A broken promise is not a floor failure. It counts in the comparison only through the rubric: if the break
  hurts what the visitor came to do, say so there.
- Where the contract is silent on an axis, there is no tell on that axis.

## The rubric: the only comparison

First fix the visitor mode from the brief. A page has one main mode; a second may be named when the brief does.

| Visitor mode | The visitor came to | Compare the variants on |
|---|---|---|
| `persuade` | decide whether to take the one action | Is the action visible without hunting at each width? Does the proof the brief names arrive before the ask? Is there one clear thing to do next in every tile? |
| `operate` | get a task done, often repeatedly | Can the main task be started from the first tile? Is state legible (what is selected, what happened)? Does the layout hold up when the content is long, empty or in error? |
| `read` | understand something | Does the first tile say what the piece answers? Can a reader find their place by headings alone? Is the text block comfortable at each width? |
| `experience` | feel or explore something | Is there one stated idea, and does each tile serve it? Does the page still say what it is and where to go? Does it stay usable with motion reduced? |

Then add the lines of the brief and of `constraints.md` that a page can meet or miss: the one action, the proof
the piece must carry, the headings it must hold.

Each rubric entry records a criterion, the visitor mode it belongs to, which variant it favours (or `none`),
and the reason, pointing at what the tiles show. Rules:

- Every criterion comes from the brief, the constraints or the table above. A criterion from the critic's own
  taste is not a rubric entry.
- Where the brief does not settle a choice and the variants differ on it, do not pick: write the question in
  `open_to_user`.
- Weigh by the brief. The count of criteria won is not the decision; a miss on the one action outweighs wins on
  lesser lines. Say which criteria decided it.
- If no variant meets a line the brief requires, the outcome is no award, naming that line.

## What tiles cannot show

Record these in `could_not_judge` rather than guessing: how motion moves, hover and focus states, anything
below a fold the capture did not tile, real content length, and load behaviour. The motion frames are a second
set of evidence for motion; they show that something moves, not whether it feels right.
