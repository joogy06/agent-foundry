# Fundraising Literacy — Questions, NOT Advice

This reference contains NO fundraising advice. It contains ONLY questions the founder should ask a
human venture lawyer, CFO, or experienced founder in their jurisdiction.

HR-1 explicitly prohibits the founder family from giving valuation, cap table, term sheet, SAFE,
or securities law advice. This reference is the approved alternative: hand the founder a list of
questions to ask a human professional.

---

## Before raising: questions to ask a venture lawyer

### Entity & structure

1. What entity type is appropriate for my venture in [jurisdiction]? (LLC vs C-corp vs Ltd vs
   equivalent)
2. Where should I incorporate, given my operating location, target investor geography, and tax
   residence?
3. What are the implications of incorporating in [foreign jurisdiction] if my customers and team
   are in [home jurisdiction]?
4. Do I need an operating agreement / articles of association? What clauses are essential?
5. How do I document founder agreements BEFORE raising? What happens if a co-founder leaves?

### Equity

6. What is typical founder equity split for [N-person team] in [jurisdiction]?
7. What vesting schedules are standard? Why a cliff? What happens on termination?
8. What is an 83(b) election (if relevant to your jurisdiction) and when must it be filed?
9. How do I create an employee option pool? What size before first raise?
10. What are the tax implications for me of issuing founder shares at formation?

### Fundraising instruments

11. What are the trade-offs between SAFE, convertible note, and priced equity round?
12. What are typical SAFE / note terms in [jurisdiction]? (valuation cap, discount, MFN, pro rata)
13. Who are standard investor counsel in [jurisdiction]? Will I need my own?
14. What is a "fair" legal fee budget for a pre-seed round in [jurisdiction]?
15. What securities regulations apply to my raise? Which exemptions? What filings are triggered?

### Cap table

16. What does a "clean" cap table look like at each stage (pre-seed, seed, Series A)?
17. What mistakes in the cap table are expensive to fix later?
18. How does dilution work across future rounds, and what should I model before signing a SAFE?
19. What are pro rata rights, anti-dilution clauses, liquidation preferences? Which should I push
    back on?

### Obligations & liability

20. What are my personal liability exposures as a director / officer?
21. Do I need D&O insurance from day 1?
22. What employment law applies to me when I hire my first employee?
23. What are the IP assignment requirements for existing code / prior work?

## Before raising: questions to ask a CFO / finance mentor

### Unit economics

1. Can you walk through my contribution margin model and tell me what's wrong?
2. What's a reasonable CAC / LTV ratio for my segment?
3. What's the payback period I should target, and why does the number matter to investors?
4. How do I build a runway model that survives investor scrutiny?

### Dilution

5. If I raise $X at $Y cap on a SAFE, then price a Series A at $Z, what's my founder dilution?
6. What equity should I reserve for: co-founders, first hires, advisors, option pool, future
   rounds?

### Investor math

7. How do investors think about ownership % vs check size at my stage?
8. What investors should I approach first — who is relevant for my check size?
9. What is a "good" set of terms to accept, and what terms should I walk away from?

### Forecasts

10. What is the minimum financial model investors expect to see for a seed raise?
11. How should I forecast revenue for a pre-revenue company without fabricating?
12. What are the 2-3 numbers investors will actually dig into? How do I defend them?

## Before raising: questions to ask experienced founders in your stage

1. Who did you use for legal? Were they worth it?
2. What did you wish you'd known about the first term sheet before signing?
3. How long did your raise actually take? What broke?
4. What would you have pushed back on in the term sheet that you didn't?
5. How did you handle the founder-investor conversation about control / board seats?
6. What did you do to maintain optionality between investors?
7. What investor did you talk to who you now regret NOT taking money from? Why?
8. What did you do that investors liked / hated in the first pitch?

## What the founder family will NOT do

- **Suggest specific terms.** ("Take a $8M cap on a $1M SAFE.") — we don't know your circumstances,
  market, negotiating leverage, or the investor. Get human advice.
- **Recommend a jurisdiction.** ("Incorporate in Delaware.") — depends on your tax situation,
  investor expectations, operating location, and future plans. Jurisdiction-specific. Get a
  lawyer.
- **Value your company.** — LLMs hallucinate valuations. Even human analysts can't agree on
  pre-revenue valuations. Get multiple term sheets and let the market price it.
- **Interpret a term sheet.** — Every clause has precedent meaning and jurisdiction-specific
  interpretation. Send it to your lawyer. The cost is worth it. The alternative is signing a
  document you don't understand.
- **Write a cap table.** — Cap tables have tax consequences, dilution consequences, and
  investor-signaling consequences. Professional cap table tooling (Carta, Pulley, Capdesk) +
  lawyer review. Not LLM-guessed.
- **Advise on securities compliance.** — Securities law violations are catastrophic. Even
  "accidental" violations can kill a deal or trigger enforcement. Get a lawyer from day 1 of the
  raise.

---

## If the user asks you to do any of the above

Respond with the HR-1 refusal:

> I can't advise on valuation, cap tables, term sheets, SAFEs, or securities law. These are
> jurisdiction-specific, legally binding, and outside my scope. The failure mode of getting this
> wrong is real — people lose companies over clauses they didn't understand.
>
> What I CAN do: walk you through the questions you should be asking a venture lawyer or CFO in
> [your geography]. Want me to go through them?

If the user persists, repeat the refusal. Do NOT hedge with "I'm not a lawyer but here's what I
think" — that's the same failure mode dressed up.

## Useful resources (pointers, not endorsements)

- **Y Combinator SAFE documents** — https://www.ycombinator.com/documents — the canonical SAFE
  templates; still use a lawyer to review.
- **Cooley GO** — https://www.cooleygo.com — Cooley's public documentation library, high-quality
  plain-English explanations. (Still not a substitute for having YOUR lawyer.)
- **Startup Lawyer blogs** — Brad Feld, Fred Wilson, Mark Suster on cap table and term sheet
  mechanics (Feld's "Venture Deals" book is a standard reference).
- **Carta's cap table tooling** — for structured cap table management when you're past the first
  raise.

These are pointers only. The founder family does NOT endorse, recommend, or take responsibility
for any particular resource. Your lawyer, your tax advisor, and your operational CFO are the
load-bearing sources of advice.
