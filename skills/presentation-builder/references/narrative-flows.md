# Narrative Flow Frameworks Reference

> 23 frameworks across 6 categories. Use the selection guide to pick the right one, then follow the slide-by-slide structure.

---

## Framework Selection Guide

| Purpose | Recommended Framework(s) |
|---|---|
| Executive recommendation / consulting deliverable | `minto-scr`, `cari`, `pyramid-principle` |
| Generic business problem/solution | `problem-solution-benefits` |
| Startup fundraising (VC pitch) | `sequoia-template`, `kawasaki-10-20-30` |
| Sales / marketing pitch | `aida`, `hook-problem-solution-proof-cta`, `pas` |
| Steering committee / project status | `status-risks-next` |
| Quick executive briefing | `bluf`, `what-so-what-now-what` |
| Architecture / design decision | `adr-presentation`, `rfc-design-review` |
| Board-level or regulator deep dive | `exec-deepdive-appendix` |
| Keynote / conference talk | `duarte-sparkline`, `heros-journey` |
| Change / transformation initiative | `before-during-after`, `freytag-pyramid` |
| Team retrospective / case study | `star-presentation`, `pixar-story-spine` |
| Lightning talk (5-7 min) | `pecha-kucha`, `ignite` |

## Category 1: Consulting / Advisory

### 1. problem-solution-benefits

**When to use:** Classic consulting engagement. Presenting a clear problem with a recommended solution and quantified benefits.
**Audience:** Senior stakeholders, steering committees, business sponsors.
**Industry:** Universal (consulting standard).

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | Project name, date, team, classification |
| 2-3 | Problem | Define the problem with data; show impact (cost, risk, time) |
| 4-5 | Solution | Proposed approach; architecture or process diagram |
| 6-7 | Benefits | Quantified ROI, efficiency gains, risk reduction |
| 8 | So What | Strategic significance; why this matters now |
| 9-10 | Next Steps | Timeline, owners, decisions required, immediate actions |

---

### 2. minto-scr

**When to use:** Persuading skeptical executives who want the answer first with logical backing. McKinsey/Minto Pyramid standard.
**Audience:** C-suite, senior partners, board members.
**Industry:** Management consulting, strategy, finance.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | Engagement name, date, confidentiality |
| 2-3 | Situation | Stable context the audience already agrees with |
| 4-5 | Complication | What changed; the tension or threat |
| 6 | Question (opt.) | The key question this deck answers |
| 7-10 | Resolution | Recommendation + 3 supporting arguments with evidence |
| 11 | Next Steps | Actions, owners, timeline, decision ask |

---

### 3. cari

**When to use:** Advisory engagements with heavy analysis. Structured for due-diligence or strategic review.
**Audience:** Advisory boards, investment committees, senior leadership.
**Industry:** Strategy consulting, M&A, financial advisory.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exec Summary | Key findings, recommendation, one-page overview |
| 2-4 | Context | Market landscape, client situation, scope of engagement |
| 5-10 | Analysis | Data-driven deep dive; 6 slides covering key dimensions |
| 11-13 | Recommendation | Options evaluated, preferred recommendation with rationale |
| 14-16 | Implementation | Roadmap, resource plan, risk mitigation, governance |
| 17+ | Appendix | Supporting data, methodology, detailed financials |

---

### 4. pyramid-principle

**When to use:** Any consulting communication. Top-down MECE structure ensures the audience gets the answer immediately.
**Audience:** Universal (executives prefer top-down; analysts need MECE).
**Industry:** All consulting firms; universal business standard.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | Topic, date, author |
| 2 | Governing Thought | Single answer/recommendation (the "so what") |
| 3-5 | Key Arguments | 3 MECE supporting pillars (one slide each) |
| 6-11 | Evidence | 2 evidence slides per argument (data, examples, proof) |
| 12 | Synthesis | Tie arguments back to governing thought |
| 13 | Next Steps | Actions required |

> **Rule:** Every slide answers "so what?" in the action title. Group arguments MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive).

## Category 2: Pitch / Sales

### 5. aida

**When to use:** Sales presentations, product launches, marketing pitches.
**Audience:** Prospects, buyers, marketing audiences.
**Industry:** Sales, marketing, B2B/B2C product launches.

| Slide # | Section | Content | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Attention | Bold stat, provocative question, or visual hook | 1 slide |
| 2-6 | Interest | Problem landscape, trends, pain points, data | 5 slides |
| 7-9 | Desire | Solution demo, testimonials, differentiation, ROI | 3 slides |
| 10 | Action | Clear CTA, pricing, next step, urgency | 1 slide |

> **Ratio rule:** A:I:D:A = 1:5:3:1 slide allocation.

---

### 6. hook-problem-solution-proof-cta

**When to use:** Startup pitch decks, investor presentations, product pitches.
**Audience:** VCs, angel investors, accelerator judges.
**Industry:** Startups, tech, venture capital.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | One-liner vision or shocking stat |
| 2-3 | Problem | Pain point with market validation |
| 4-5 | Solution | Product/service with demo or screenshot |
| 6-7 | Proof | Traction metrics, testimonials, pilot results |
| 8 | Market | TAM/SAM/SOM with credible sizing |
| 9 | Business Model | Revenue model, unit economics |
| 10 | Team | Founders, advisors, key hires |
| 11 | Ask | Funding amount, use of proceeds, timeline |
| 12+ | Appendix | Detailed financials, tech architecture, competitive matrix |

---

### 7. pas

**When to use:** Aggressive persuasion where the audience needs to feel urgency before hearing the solution.
**Audience:** Buyers sitting on the fence, budget holders resistant to change.
**Industry:** Sales, consulting change management, crisis response.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | Topic framed as a challenge |
| 2-3 | Problem | Define the pain; show who is affected |
| 4-6 | Agitate | Amplify consequences: cost of inaction, competitor moves, risk escalation, emotional impact |
| 7-9 | Solution | Relief: how the solution eliminates the pain; proof it works |
| 10 | CTA | Specific ask with urgency trigger |

> **Key:** The Agitate phase is what differentiates PAS. It makes the audience uncomfortable before offering relief.

---

### 8. kawasaki-10-20-30

**When to use:** VC pitches with strict time/slide limits. Guy Kawasaki's rule.
**Audience:** Venture capitalists, angel investors.
**Industry:** Tech startups.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | Company name, tagline, contact |
| 2 | Problem/Opportunity | The pain you solve |
| 3 | Value Proposition | Your unique solution in one sentence |
| 4 | Secret Sauce | Underlying technology or unfair advantage |
| 5 | Business Model | How you make money |
| 6 | Go-to-Market | Customer acquisition strategy |
| 7 | Competition | Landscape positioning (avoid 2x2 matrix cliches) |
| 8 | Team | Key people and relevant experience |
| 9 | Financials | 3-5 year projections, key assumptions |
| 10 | The Ask | Amount, use of funds, milestones |

> **Rules:** Exactly 10 slides. 20 minutes max. 30pt font minimum (forces brevity).

---

### 9. sequoia-template

**When to use:** The gold-standard VC pitch template used by Sequoia Capital portfolio companies.
**Audience:** Series A+ venture capitalists.
**Industry:** Tech startups, high-growth ventures.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Company Purpose | One-sentence mission |
| 2 | Problem | Describe the pain |
| 3 | Solution | Your product/approach |
| 4 | Why Now | Market timing, trends enabling this |
| 5 | Market Size | TAM/SAM/SOM with bottom-up sizing |
| 6 | Competition | Landscape and your differentiation |
| 7 | Product | Screenshots, demo, architecture |
| 8 | Business Model | Revenue streams, pricing, unit economics |
| 9 | Team | Founders, key hires, advisors |
| 10 | Financials | Revenue, burn rate, projections |
| 11 | The Deal | Round size, valuation, use of proceeds |
| 12 | Vision | Where this goes in 5-10 years |

## Category 3: Status / Governance

### 10. status-risks-next

**When to use:** Steering committee meetings, monthly governance reviews, PMO reporting.
**Audience:** Steering committee, PMO, senior sponsors.
**Industry:** Enterprise IT, banking programme governance, large-scale projects.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exec Summary + RAG | Overall RAG status, headline metrics, key message |
| 2 | Decisions Required | Items needing steering committee action (table format) |
| 3-4 | Status Dashboard | Workstream RAG, milestones achieved, % complete |
| 5-6 | Milestones | Timeline/Gantt with actuals vs plan |
| 7-8 | Issues & Risks | Top 5 risks (probability/impact), top 5 issues, mitigations |
| 9 | Budget | Spend vs forecast, variance, EAC |
| 10 | Recommendation | Proposed path forward |
| 11-12 | Next Steps | Actions, owners, dates for next period |

> **Max 10-12 slides.** Anything more goes in appendix. Pre-read the deck; meeting time is for decisions.

---

### 11. bluf

**When to use:** Military-style briefings. When the audience has 2 minutes and needs the answer immediately.
**Audience:** Time-pressed executives, military/government, incident response.
**Industry:** Government, defence, banking operations, crisis management.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bottom Line | The answer, decision, or recommendation (one slide) |
| 2-3 | Supporting Facts | Key evidence supporting the bottom line |
| 4 | Action Required | What you need from the audience, by when |
| 5 | Background (opt.) | Context for those who need it; skip if audience is briefed |

> **Rule:** If the audience walks out after slide 1, they have everything they need.

---

### 12. what-so-what-now-what

**When to use:** Quick briefings, status updates, incident debriefs.
**Audience:** Mid-senior management, cross-functional teams.
**Industry:** Universal. Popular in agile, military, healthcare.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | Topic, date |
| 2-3 | What | Facts, data, observations (what happened or what we found) |
| 4-5 | So What | Why it matters; implications, risks, opportunities |
| 6-7 | Now What | Recommended actions, owners, timeline |

## Category 4: Technical

### 13. adr-presentation

**When to use:** Presenting an architecture decision to a review board or engineering leadership.
**Audience:** Architecture review boards, tech leads, engineering VPs.
**Industry:** Software engineering, platform teams.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title + Status | ADR number, title, status (Proposed/Accepted/Deprecated) |
| 2-3 | Context | Business and technical context driving the decision |
| 4-5 | Options Considered | Each option with pros/cons (table or matrix) |
| 6 | Decision + Rationale | Chosen option and why |
| 7 | Consequences | Trade-offs accepted; what changes as a result |
| 8 | Implementation | High-level plan, migration steps |
| 9 | References | Related ADRs, RFCs, documentation links |

---

### 14. rfc-design-review

**When to use:** Technical proposals for new systems, major refactors, or cross-team platform changes.
**Audience:** Engineering teams, principal engineers, CTOs.
**Industry:** Software engineering, infrastructure, platform teams.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | RFC title, author, date, status |
| 2 | Problem Statement | What problem this solves; impact if unsolved |
| 3 | Goals / Non-Goals | Explicit scope boundaries |
| 4-5 | Proposed Design | Architecture diagrams, data flow, API contracts |
| 6 | Alternatives Considered | Other approaches and why they were rejected |
| 7 | Trade-offs & Risks | Performance, security, operational complexity |
| 8 | Migration / Rollout | Phased rollout, feature flags, rollback plan |
| 9 | Open Questions | Unresolved items for discussion |
| 10 | Timeline | Milestones, dependencies, delivery estimate |

---

### 15. exec-deepdive-appendix

**When to use:** Board presentations, regulator briefings, investor deep-dives in banking/finance. Heavy appendix model.
**Audience:** Board of directors, regulators, institutional investors.
**Industry:** Banking, finance, insurance, regulated industries.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1-3 | Exec Summary | Key messages, strategic context, headline metrics |
| 4-5 | Situation / Market | Market conditions, competitive landscape, macro trends |
| 6-15 | Deep Dive | Detailed analysis across 10 slides (financials, ops, risk, compliance) |
| 16-17 | Recommendation | Strategic options, preferred path, rationale |
| 18-37+ | Appendix | 20+ slides: detailed data tables, methodology, regulatory references, scenario analysis |

> **Convention:** Main deck is 15-17 slides. Appendix can be 20-50+ slides. Presenters reference appendix slides by number during Q&A.

## Category 5: Storytelling / Transformation

### 16. heros-journey

**When to use:** Keynotes, transformation narratives, leadership offsites. 3-act story structure adapted for business.
**Audience:** Large audiences, all-hands, conference attendees.
**Industry:** Leadership communications, change management, keynotes.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Act 1: Ordinary World | Current state; "life before" |
| 3 | Call to Adventure | The opportunity or threat that demands change |
| 4 | Resistance | Why change is hard; objections, fear, inertia |
| 5 | Mentor/Guide | Who or what enables the journey (your solution, leader, partner) |
| 6-7 | Act 2: Challenges | Trials faced during transformation |
| 8 | Ordeal | The hardest moment; the turning point |
| 9 | Reward | Breakthrough achieved; proof of progress |
| 10-11 | Act 3: Return | New world; transformed state with evidence |
| 12 | Call to Action | Audience's role in the next chapter |

---

### 17. before-during-after

**When to use:** Transformation programmes, change management, digital transformation narratives.
**Audience:** Sponsors, programme teams, affected business units.
**Industry:** Change management, IT transformation, organisational design.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1-3 | Before | Legacy state, pain points, limitations, failed attempts |
| 4-7 | During | Transformation journey: approach, milestones, challenges overcome, key decisions |
| 8-10 | After | New capabilities, measured outcomes, user feedback |
| 11 | Sustaining Change | Governance, adoption metrics, continuous improvement |
| 12 | Next Phase | What comes next; scaling, adjacent opportunities |

---

### 18. duarte-sparkline

**When to use:** Keynote speeches, visionary presentations. Nancy Duarte's framework alternating between current and future states.
**Audience:** Large audiences, conferences, company-wide events.
**Industry:** Keynotes, thought leadership, product launches.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | What Is | Current reality; status quo |
| 3-4 | What Could Be | Vision of the future; contrast with present |
| 5-6 | What Is | Back to current pain; reinforce the gap |
| 7-8 | What Could Be | Deeper vision; show it's achievable |
| 9 | STAR Moment | Something They'll Always Remember (demo, reveal, story) |
| 10-11 | What Could Be | Full picture of the new world |
| 12 | New Bliss / CTA | The call to cross over; action the audience can take |

> **Key:** The alternation between "is" and "could be" builds emotional momentum. The STAR moment is the peak.

---

### 19. pixar-story-spine

**When to use:** Making dry business content engaging. Case studies, product narratives, team retrospectives.
**Audience:** Mixed audiences, internal communications, customer stories.
**Industry:** Universal storytelling framework.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Once upon a time... | The initial situation; how things were |
| 2-3 | Every day... | The routine; the status quo |
| 4 | One day... | The inciting incident; what changed |
| 5-6 | Because of that... | Chain of consequences (repeat as needed) |
| 7 | Until finally... | The resolution; the outcome |
| 8 | And ever since then... | The new normal; lasting impact |
| 9 | The moral | Key takeaway; lesson learned |

---

### 20. freytag-pyramid

**When to use:** Longer narratives, transformation case studies, dramatic product reveals.
**Audience:** Conferences, offsites, storytelling-heavy presentations.
**Industry:** Universal dramatic structure.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Exposition | Setting, characters, context; establish the world |
| 3-5 | Rising Action | Escalating challenges, complications, tension building |
| 6-7 | Climax | The turning point; peak tension, critical decision |
| 8-9 | Falling Action | Consequences of the climax; resolution taking shape |
| 10-11 | Denouement | New equilibrium; lessons, outcomes, future state |

## Category 6: Lightning / Constrained

### 21. pecha-kucha

**When to use:** Creative showcases, design reviews, community events. Auto-advancing format forces discipline.
**Audience:** Creative communities, design events, informal showcases.
**Industry:** Design, architecture, creative industries.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1-20 | 20 slides x 20 sec | Image-dominant slides; auto-advance; 6 min 40 sec total |

> **Rules:** No bullet points. One image per slide. Narrative arc across 20 slides. Rehearse with a timer. No pausing or going back.

**Suggested arc:** Slides 1-3 (context), 4-7 (problem/journey), 8-14 (development/exploration), 15-18 (results/impact), 19-20 (reflection/CTA).

---

### 22. ignite

**When to use:** Tech community lightning talks. Tighter than Pecha Kucha.
**Audience:** Tech meetups, unconferences, developer events.
**Industry:** Technology, open source, developer communities.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1-20 | 20 slides x 15 sec | Auto-advance; 5 minutes total; visual-heavy |

> **Rules:** 15 seconds per slide (not 20). Total 5 min. Auto-advance is mandatory. Humour encouraged. Rehearse relentlessly.

**Suggested arc:** Slides 1-2 (hook), 3-5 (problem), 6-12 (solution/demo), 13-17 (evidence/story), 18-19 (impact), 20 (CTA/punchline).

---

### 23. star-presentation

**When to use:** Case studies, project retrospectives, lessons-learned presentations, interview presentations.
**Audience:** Peers, leadership reviews, interview panels.
**Industry:** Universal. Common in consulting, banking, HR processes.

| Slide # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | Case/project name, date, presenter |
| 2-3 | Situation | Business context, team, constraints, starting conditions |
| 4 | Task | Specific objective or challenge assigned |
| 5-7 | Action | What was done: approach, decisions, execution details |
| 8-9 | Result | Quantified outcomes, before/after comparison |
| 10 | Learnings | Key takeaways, what would be done differently |

## Consulting Slide Conventions

These conventions apply across all frameworks but are mandatory in consulting/advisory contexts.

### Action Titles
Every slide title is a complete sentence stating the slide's "so what." The audience should be able to read only the titles and understand the full argument.

- **Bad:** "Market Overview"
- **Good:** "The UK SMB market grew 12% YoY, creating a $2B addressable opportunity"

### Horizontal Flow (Storyline)
Reading only the slide titles in sequence tells a coherent, logical story. Test this by copying all titles into a single document and reading top-to-bottom.

### Vertical Flow (Slide Logic)
Within each slide: the action title (claim) is supported by the body content (evidence). Title asserts; body proves. No title should make a claim the body cannot substantiate.

### MECE Grouping
Arguments and sections must be Mutually Exclusive (no overlap) and Collectively Exhaustive (no gaps). If you have 3 pillars, they must cover the full space without redundancy.

### Ghost Deck / Dot-Dash
Before building slides, write a "ghost deck" (also called dot-dash outline): each slide as one title sentence + 3-5 bullet points of intended content. Review the ghost deck storyline before designing a single slide.

### Source Lines
Every data point, chart, and external claim must have a source line at the bottom of the slide. Format: `Source: [Author/Org], [Publication], [Date]`. Internal data: `Source: Internal analysis, [Dataset], [Date]`.

### Callout Boxes
Use callout boxes (highlighted text boxes) to draw attention to the single most important insight on a slide. Limit to one per slide. Place in a consistent location (typically bottom-right or right margin).
