# Confirmation statement (CS01) — a separate annual obligation

<!-- REVIEW-BY: 2027-04-06 -->
**REVIEW-BY: 2027-04-06** · verified 2026-07-29.

**This is not part of the accounts filing, and missing that is why it gets filed late.** Accounts go
to Companies House on the ARD cycle; the confirmation statement runs on its own **review period** and
its own date. A company that files perfect accounts can still be in default here.

## What it is

An annual **check and confirm** that the public register is correct. It is not a return of activity
or of finances — it carries no figures from the accounts.

- **Deadline:** within **14 days** of the end of the review period.
- **Review period:** 12 months from incorporation, then 12 months from the date of the last statement.
- **You may file early**, which resets the review period — useful when you want the register updated
  immediately after a change.
- **One is due even if nothing has changed.** "Nothing to report" is still a filing, and its absence
  is still a default.

## What you confirm or update

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Registered office address | Must be an **appropriate address** where post reaches someone |
| Registered email address | **Required since 4 March 2024** — not published; Companies House uses it to contact the company |
| **Statement of lawful purpose** | **Required annually since 5 March 2024** — confirm intended future activities are lawful. **The statement cannot be filed without it.** |
| Directors and secretaries | Appointments, resignations, changed details |
| **People with Significant Control (PSC)** | The most commonly wrong section — see below |
| SIC codes | What the company actually does now, not at incorporation |
| Statement of capital | Shares issued, classes, aggregate nominal value, amounts paid/unpaid |
| Shareholders | For most small companies, a full list at least every third statement, with changes in between |
| Register locations / election to hold on the public register | If applicable |

**Some changes cannot be made on the CS01 itself** — a director appointment or resignation, a
registered office move, or an allotment of shares are filed on their **own forms** and the
confirmation statement then confirms the resulting position. Trying to "correct" the register through
the CS01 alone leaves it wrong.

## PSC — where small companies actually go wrong

A PSC is broadly someone who holds more than 25% of shares or voting rights, can appoint or remove a
majority of the board, or otherwise exercises significant influence or control.

Recurring errors:
- **A sole director/shareholder forgetting they are the PSC.** They are, and the register must say so.
- **Not updating after a share transfer** that crosses the 25% boundary.
- **Recording a corporate shareholder as a PSC** when the rules on relevant legal entities apply
  differently — check before entering it.
- **Treating PSC as a one-off at incorporation.** It is a live register; changes are notifiable
  within their own deadlines, *separately* from and usually sooner than the annual statement.

## Identity verification (ECCTA)

Companies House began rolling out **identity verification from 18 November 2025** under the Economic
Crime and Corporate Transparency Act. Directors and PSCs are being brought into scope on a transition
timetable, and filings will increasingly require a verified identity.

**Check the current position before assuming a filing can be made** — this is actively changing, and
an unverified individual can find a routine filing blocked.

## Common failures

- Believing it is filed with, or as part of, the annual accounts.
- Missing it because nothing changed that year.
- Letting the review period drift after filing an early statement.
- Leaving the PSC register stale after a share transfer.
- Stale SIC codes describing a business the company no longer runs.
- Assuming the registered email address is optional, or public. It is neither.
