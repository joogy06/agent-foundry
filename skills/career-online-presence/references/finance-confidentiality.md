# Regulated-Employee Publishing Checklist

The mandatory compliance gate for `career-online-presence` HARD RULE 2. Written generally enough for any regulated sector, with **finance as the worked example**. This is a *checklist to run before recommending any public posting* — it is not legal advice, and the standing instruction is: **when in doubt, route to the user's compliance / legal function for pre-clearance.**

> This file documents a mechanism, not a statistic. The regulatory citations are in `~/.claude/skills/career-coach/references/market-snapshot-2026-06.md`.

---

## Run this BEFORE any posting plan

Confirm the user's status against each policy class. If any is unclear, the answer is **pre-clearance**, not "post it".

### Policy classes

1. **Employer social-media / external-communications policy.** Most regulated employers have one. It may require pre-approval of any public professional posting, ban certain topics, or mandate disclaimers. Read it first; it governs everything below.

2. **FINRA Rule 2210 (communications with the public).** If the user is a registered person (or their posting could be construed as a firm communication), public content about business may be a "retail communication" subject to content standards, supervision, and recordkeeping. Personal posts can cross into this when they touch the firm's business or products.

3. **SEC personal-communications expectations.** Registered persons' business-related communications on personal accounts/devices can fall under firm books-and-records and supervision obligations. Treat business-touching posts as potentially recordable.

4. **MNPI (material non-public information).** Never post anything that could constitute or signal MNPI — deal flow, unannounced results, client positions, anything price-sensitive and not public.

5. **Client confidentiality.** No client names, no identifiable client situations, no engagement details.

6. **Internal-systems disclosure.** No proprietary architecture, internal tool names, security controls, or anything that maps the firm's internal estate.

7. **Performance / return claims & testimonials.** Performance claims and testimonials are heavily regulated (and often outright restricted for individuals). Do not post returns, track records, or testimonial-style endorsements.

8. **Personal-account disclaimers & affiliation disclosure.** Where the user posts about their field, disclose affiliation as policy requires and use any mandated "views are my own" disclaimer — and know that a disclaimer does NOT exempt business communications from supervision.

9. **Pre-clearance workflow.** If the firm has a pre-clearance process for outside communications, route through it. Document approvals.

## Decision rule

```
Is the user in a regulated role / registered person / under an external-comms policy?
   │
   ├── No  → proceed with the general presence plan (still apply privacy + no-fabrication rules).
   │
   └── Yes → for EACH planned posting:
              • Does it touch the firm's business, products, clients, or performance?
              • Could it carry MNPI or map internal systems?
              • Does policy require pre-approval / disclaimers?
              → If any "yes" or "unsure": PRE-CLEARANCE before publishing. Do not advise posting on assumption.
```

## What this skill will and won't do

- **Will:** flag the gate, walk the checklist, help structure compliant, non-sensitive professional content (e.g., generic domain expertise with no client/firm specifics, cleared and disclaimed).
- **Won't:** advise how to skirt a policy, downplay the gate, or treat a disclaimer as a free pass. The compliant path is the only path offered.

## Generalizing beyond finance

The same structure applies to other regulated sectors (healthcare/HIPAA, legal privilege, defense/ITAR, public-company insiders): identify the governing policy, the confidential-information class, the disclosure/pre-clearance requirements, and route uncertainty to compliance. Finance (FINRA/SEC/MNPI) is the worked example because it is the family's first-class vertical.
